# Describe It. Prompt It. Reflect On It

## Original idea

**Food Adventure: A Journey Through the Human Body** is an interactive learning website for children ages 8–12. It makes the digestive process visible by following one bite of food through the mouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, and large intestine.
The goal is to help learners understand three connected ideas: where each digestive organ is located in the body, what each organ does, and how food changes as it moves through the system. The website uses a colorful human-body illustration, labelled organs, short explanations, and controllable animations.

## Instructions for Opening or Running the Project

1. Download or clone this GitHub repository to your local computer.
2. Locate the file named `index.html`.
3. Double-click `index.html`, or right-click it and choose a web browser such as Chrome or Safari.
4. Use **Next**, **Previous**, **Pause/Resume**, and **Replay stage** to explore the five stages of digestion.
5. Click an organ in the body illustration to revisit that organ's stage.

No installation, build tools, accounts, or external services are required.

## AI Tools Used & Selected Prompts

**AI Tools Used:** Codex + ChatGPT

### (1) Selected Prompt 1: Initial learning experience

> I want to create an interactive animation about how food travels through the human digestive system. Create a colorful, friendly, and easy-to-understand experience for children, using one `index.html` file with CSS and JavaScript inside it.

### (2) Selected Prompt 2: More meaningful interactions

> I want to show more details about what happens when food travels through different organs: chewing and saliva in the mouth, muscle movement in the esophagus, mixing with digestive juices in the stomach, nutrient absorption in the small intestine, and water absorption in the large intestine.

### (3) Selected Prompt 3: Clear anatomy and learner controls

> Make sure the mouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, and large intestine are clearly visible and positioned according to their actual locations in the human body. Add labels with connecting lines, highlight one organ at a time, and allow learners to use Next, Previous, Pause, and Replay buttons.

## Reflection

This project showed me that communicating with AI is an iterative design process. I began with a broad idea about making digestion visible, but I needed to give more specific prompts after reviewing each version. For example, I explained that the target learners are children ages 8–12, that the digestive organs must stay connected in realistic body locations, and that the interactions should support learning instead of becoming distracting games. Giving focused feedback helped transform a simple food-moving animation into a structured learning experience.

AI was useful as a coding and brainstorming partner. It helped me create the single-file website, organize the stages, and generate animation ideas. However, I still needed to decide what was educationally important. I chose to focus on five key organs and five main changes in food: chewing, muscular pushing, mixing into chyme, nutrient absorption, and water absorption. I also decided that the full digestive system should remain visible during every stage so learners can understand how the organs are related.

Computational Thinking helped me make the biological process easier to design and explain. I used **decomposition** by dividing the complex digestive system into five smaller stages. I used **sequencing** by placing the organs in the order that food travels: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. I used **abstraction** by showing the most important changes without trying to represent every cell, enzyme, or blood vessel in a real human body. Finally, I used **algorithmic thinking** by giving every stage the same structure: select and highlight an organ, play its animation, explain the change in food, and let the learner pause or replay it.

One limitation is that the anatomy is simplified for young learners rather than medically detailed. In a future version, I would add an optional short knowledge check after each stage, audio narration, and a choice of different foods to compare how they change during digestion.
